What to say, briefly

  • Answer to the research question: structural chunking (heading 0.839, paragraph 0.746) beats fixed-length (best 0.576); sentence-level semantic splitting fails (0.034) because the evidence is not retrieved, not because of the completeness rule: coverage is scored on the union of retrieved chunks, and union character coverage is only 0.272 against 0.767 for heading.
  • Reranking helps but is not established here: +0.047 on test with a CI that includes zero; it does raise Recall@3 by +0.058 (Holm p = 0.014) at a fixed pool and cuts the matched-budget gap. On test2 the direction repeated: +0.052 [+0.020, +0.089] — positive, but the size is unresolved at 45 groups.
  • The chunking conclusion does not depend on reranking: rank correlation 0.963, so the recommendation is stable.
  • Disclosure: the test split had already been used for a pilot, so those are re-evaluations of a seen split, not a pristine holdout. test2 is the opposite case — never used before — but it was drafted and reviewed by the agent, not by a human.
